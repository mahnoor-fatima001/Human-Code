LEARNING PATH

Cognitive Biases

Discover the mental shortcuts and systematic patterns that can influence judgment.

12 lessonsSelf-pacedBeginner friendly
How to use this path

Move through the lessons in order, take notes, and test your understanding with reflection and knowledge checks.

CURRICULUM

Lessons

01

What Is a Cognitive Bias?

Learn what cognitive biases are, why mental shortcuts can be useful, and when they can distort judgment.

02

Confirmation Bias

Notice how people may seek, interpret, or remember evidence in ways that support an existing belief.

03

Anchoring

Explore how an initial number or impression can influence later estimates, even when that starting point is arbitrary.

04

Availability Heuristic

Understand why examples that come easily to mind can seem more common or likely than they really are.

05

Hindsight Bias

Examine why outcomes can feel more predictable after they happen and how this can distort learning from experience.

06

Halo Effect

Learn how one noticeable positive or negative impression can spill over into judgments about unrelated traits.

07

Framing Effect

Compare how different presentations of the same information can shift attention and influence a decision.

08

Loss Aversion

Explore why potential losses can feel more powerful than equivalent gains and how this may affect choices.

09

Sunk Cost Fallacy

Recognize when past time, money, or effort influences a decision that should be based on future costs and benefits.

10

Fundamental Attribution Error

Examine the tendency to overemphasize personal traits and underweight situational factors when judging others.

11

Self-Serving Bias

Explore how people may explain success and failure in ways that protect their self-image.

12

Dunning-Kruger Effect

Learn about the relationship between skill, self-assessment, and confidence while avoiding simplistic interpretations of the effect.

Educational note: Human behavior is complex. These lessons are intended to build understanding and critical thinking, not to diagnose people or provide instructions for exploiting them.
